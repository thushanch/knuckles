Plate01Structure

Most diagrams show you about a fifth of it.

The clitoris is roughly 9 to 10 centimetres of erectile tissue. Most of it is internal â€” two legs running back along the pubic arch, and two bulbs wrapping the vaginal opening. All of it engorges. All of it is innervated.

Plate02Notation

We draw technique the way physiotherapy draws movement.

No bodies, no performers, nothing to compare yourself to. A technique is four variables: a region, a path, a pressure, and a tempo. Change one and the rest of the diagram responds â€” which is the actual lesson, because in practice you are adjusting one variable at a time and reading the response.

The dotted contours are sensitivity gradient, not outline. Density of innervation is why the same path at a different pressure is a different technique.

Plate03Response

Desire does not always come first.

The model most people carry â€” desire, then arousal, then orgasm, in that order â€” comes from research published in 1966. For a great many women it describes the experience badly, and the mismatch gets read as a personal failing.

The responsive model puts arousal before desire: something starts, the body responds, and wanting arrives afterwards. Nothing is broken. The sequence is simply different.
